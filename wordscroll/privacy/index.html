WordScroll — Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 25, 2026
Last updated: November 10, 2026
Published by: Henry Truman Cunningham, United States
Contact: wordscroll-support@jelly.fish

1. The short version

WordScroll has no accounts and no sign-in, so there is nothing here that identifies you. Your words, goal, streak, saved cards and settings are stored on your device. Four things leave it: the purchase check the app store needs (§4), the app's requests to our word server, and two kinds of anonymous report, usage counts and crash reports (§5). At launch the app asks for definition updates (corrections to the words it carries), sending only this build's content id and the number of the last update it holds; the first time you meet a word, it downloads that word's recorded pronunciation. Neither request carries an account or a device identifier. Both are received by Supabase, our processor, which runs the server in Canada and whose request logs keep the caller's IP address and the time of each request for seven days, to run and secure the service. Nothing uses them to identify you or to work out where you are.

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with data brokers. We run no advertising, and the app contains no advertising SDK.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you make in the app is device-local:

  • the words you have seen, saved or marked as known;
  • your daily goal, level and chosen word packs;
  • your streak, quiz history and daily progress;
  • your theme, voice accent and word-drop schedule;
  • the sentences you say in In a Sentence, and their marks (each sentence goes out once, to be marked, and comes straight back: see section 3);
  • how far you got in onboarding.

There is no server of ours holding a copy, and nothing moves between your devices on its own. Like other app data, it is included when your phone saves a copy of itself to iCloud or a computer, and setting up a new phone from that copy brings it back. We never see that copy. Deleting the app removes it from your phone.

3. What we never ask for

The app does not ask for your name, email address, phone number or contacts. It does not read your location, photos, camera, health data or address book. It never asks for permission to read or use any of them, so iOS does not give it access.

The one thing it listens to is you, in In a Sentence, a practice mode in WordScroll Plus: you tap the mic and say a sentence with the day's word. The first time, iOS asks whether WordScroll may use the microphone and speech recognition, and you can say no and type the sentence instead. Your phone turns what you say into text with Apple's speech recognition, on the phone where it can and otherwise on Apple's servers under Apple's own privacy policy. WordScroll never records the audio, and no audio leaves the app. The written sentence, the word and its dictionary definition are sent to our marking service, which has an AI model (Anthropic's Claude, through Anthropic's API) mark the sentence, and the mark comes back to your phone. Anthropic, as the model's provider, keeps what the API is sent for up to 30 days under its API data retention policy, longer only where the law or its safety review requires, and never to train its models. The sentence is used to answer you and is not stored by us; what our service keeps is a count of sentences a day for each installation, so the feature cannot be run up, and nothing that identifies you. The microphone is on only while you are saying a sentence.

It can add one thing to your photos, and only when you ask: share a word card, choose Save Image in the share sheet, and iOS asks you once whether WordScroll may add to your photos. That lets it save the picture; it never lets it see your library. Apple also requires the app to carry a second photo-library permission text because of a library it ships; the app never uses that permission, never asks for it, and the text says so.

4. Purchases

Payment is handled entirely by the app store. WordScroll never sees a card number, a name or a billing address.

We use RevenueCat, Inc. (https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy) to ask the store two questions: whether the store account currently running the app owns WordScroll Plus, and whether an introductory offer is available to it.

  • What is sent: the app store's own purchase receipt and an anonymous purchase identifier that RevenueCat generates for the installation. We do not supply a user id, because the app has none.
  • What it is used for: unlocking Plus features, restoring purchases on a new device, and deciding whether to show trial wording. Nothing else.
  • What it is not used for: advertising, profiling, or tracking you across other apps or websites.
  • Retention: RevenueCat retains subscriber and transaction records for the life of the app account and as required for tax and audit purposes; see their privacy policy for detail. Deleting the app does not delete the purchase record, because the purchase belongs to your app store account — which is also what makes "Restore purchases" work.

5. Usage analytics and crash reports

The app sends two kinds of report. Neither carries your name, an email address, an account or anything you have written in the app.

Usage counts. The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase, from Google, to count how WordScroll is used: how far the first-run questions go, which features are used (reading, saving and practicing words, sharing, reminders, the plans screen) and whether a day's goal is met. Each report carries a random number Firebase makes for this installation of the app, the device model, the system version and the app version. Google works out an approximate location (country and region) from the connection's IP address. The app never sets a user id, there is no account to link the reports to, advertising and ad-personalization signals are switched off at every launch, and the app does not read the advertising identifier (IDFA). Google receives the reports as our processor.

Crash reports. When the app hits an error, it sends a report to Sentry, our processor: what went wrong and where in the code, the app version, the device model and system version, and the app's own steps just before the error. The report carries no user id, and Sentry is set not to store IP addresses.

There is no tracking-permission prompt, because none of this tracks you (§7).

6. Notifications

Word drops and streak reminders are scheduled on your device by the operating system. Nothing about them is sent anywhere: there is no push server, no push token leaves the device, and the app never asks for an email address. You can turn them off in the app or in iOS Settings at any time, and the app keeps working exactly as before.

7. Tracking and advertising

We do not track you as Apple defines it: we do not link data from this app with third-party data for advertising or advertising measurement, and we share nothing with data brokers. The app therefore never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, and it does not read the advertising identifier (IDFA).

8. Children

WordScroll is not directed at children and has no child-specific features. It asks no one, children included, for personal information, and the reports in §5 carry no name, email address or account. It has no chat, no user-to-user content and no user profiles.

9. Your data rights

Because we hold no personal data about you, most access, correction and portability requests have nothing to act on — there is no record keyed to you.

  • Deleting your data: delete the app. Everything in §2 goes with it.
  • The word server's request logs (§1) keep an IP address for seven days and then delete it. They are not linked to anything that names you, so there is no record in them to find or correct.
  • Usage counts and crash reports (§5) carry a random installation number, not your name or an account, so there is no record in them we could find as yours to correct or delete. Google and Sentry keep them for the retention period each service is set to, and then delete them.
  • Purchase records are held by the app store and by RevenueCat as processor; to have them removed, contact us at the address at the top of this page and we will pass the request to RevenueCat.
  • If you are in the EEA/UK, the lawful basis for the §4 purchase check is performance of a contract (delivering the subscription you bought), and for the §5 reports it is our legitimate interest in finding faults and improving the app; if you are in California, we do not "sell" or "share" personal information as the CCPA/CPRA define those terms.
  • If you are in Canada, you can ask what personal information we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected, and withdraw consent, at the address in §12. In Québec, the person responsible for protecting personal information is named there too.

10. International transfers

RevenueCat processes data on servers in the United States. If you use the app elsewhere, the purchase check described in §4 is transferred there. Our word server (§1) runs in Canada, in Supabase's Canada Central region, so its requests and their logs are processed there wherever you use the app. Google processes the usage counts, and Sentry the crash reports (§5), on servers in the United States.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version ships with an app update — the copy inside the app is part of the binary, so what you read in the app always matches the version you are running. The date at the top of this page reflects the last change to the hosted copy.
